BOUNCY-CLIMBER
GAME IMPROVEMENT REVIEW AND MEETING NOTES
==========================================================================

Purpose: Discuss how to make the game more approachable for children and
playable on Android phones, iPhones, tablets, and computers.

Basis: Recommendations from the earlier conversation, based on the uploaded
index.html, style.css, and script.js.

Testing scope recorded in the original review: Chromium with keyboard,
mouse, and emulated touch at phone, tablet, and desktop viewport sizes.
Those observations are reproduced here; this document is not a new test
pass. Physical Android devices, iPhones, and iPads still need testing.
Suggested dimensions and difficulty values are starting points for
playtesting, not final balance decisions.


1. OVERALL VERDICT
==========================================================================

The core idea is workable: an automatically bouncing character that the
player steers left and right gives the game a simple foundation.

The current version feels more like a mechanics prototype than a finished
game. The three biggest issues are:

1) Controls are invisible and unexplained.
2) The fixed 400 x 600 layout is cropped or undersized on several devices.
3) Once the player understands the mechanic, there is little variety,
   progression, or reward.

Main recommendation: Do not prioritize extra artwork first. Make the
controls understandable, make the whole game visible on every screen,
and make the first few successful landings easy and rewarding.


2. DEVICE OBSERVATIONS FROM THE EARLIER REVIEW
==========================================================================

Small phone: 320 x 568
- The game extended approximately 16 pixels beyond both the top and bottom.
- The Home button was partly offscreen.
- The 400-pixel game width was squeezed to 320 pixels while the height
  remained 600 pixels.

Modern phone: 390 x 844
- The game was fully visible and basically playable.
- The play area remained only 600 pixels tall, leaving large empty bars
  above and below it.

Landscape phone: 844 x 390
- Approximately 105 pixels were cropped from the top and bottom.
- The score, Home button, lower platforms, and floor could disappear.
- This orientation needs an explicit layout solution.

Tablet: 820 x 1180
- The game remained 400 x 600.
- It looked unnecessarily small in the middle of the screen.

Computer: 1366 x 768
- The game fit, and the arrow keys worked.
- The presentation looked sparse, and mouse controls were not obvious.

Implementation issue identified in the review:
The canvas and container use a fixed 400 x 600 layout with centered
positioning and overflow hidden. Width can shrink without proportional
height adjustment, leading to distortion or clipping.


3. MAKE THE CONTROLS OBVIOUS
==========================================================================

Current problem
---------------
The start screen only says "Tap to Start." It does not explain that:
- The character jumps automatically.
- Players must hold the left or right half to move.
- Desktop players can use the arrow keys.
- The character wraps around the sides of the screen.

Movement starts on touchstart or mousedown and stops on release. In the
earlier touch test, a quick tap produced essentially no movement, while a
sustained press worked. Sliding a finger across the screen does not change
direction until the player lifts it.

Recommended changes
-------------------
Add a short interactive tutorial:

    Hold the left or right side to move.
    Keyboard: Left / Right arrows or A / D.
    Your character jumps automatically!

Show two large translucent arrow zones during the first few jumps. Fade
them after the player has landed successfully two or three times.

Prototype direct horizontal dragging as an alternative touch scheme:
the character follows the finger smoothly. Test this against hold-to-move
controls with the intended age group rather than assuming either scheme
will suit every child.

Explain or demonstrate side wrapping so it is not a surprise.


4. REDESIGN THE OPENING 30 SECONDS
==========================================================================

Current problem
---------------
The character begins in mid-air and is already jumping. A full-width floor
catches it. Without useful steering, it can repeatedly bounce on that
floor while the score stays at zero.

Scoring only begins when the character climbs above the vertical midpoint.
That can make the opening look unresponsive or confusing.

Recommended opening
-------------------
1) Start the character visibly on a wide platform.
2) Show a short control demonstration, approximately three seconds.
3) Put the first platform almost directly above the character.
4) Make the first three to five landings very easy.
5) Award the first point or star immediately after the first landing.

The player should understand the basic action and experience a small
success before facing a difficult jump.


5. DESIGN DIFFICULTY INSTEAD OF RELYING ON RANDOMNESS
==========================================================================

Current problem
---------------
Every platform is 60 x 10, vertical spacing is 80, and horizontal positions
are selected independently at random. The character is only 20 x 20.

This can make the opening inconsistent between runs. Platform size and
spacing also do not gradually teach the player new challenges.

Recommended changes
-------------------
Use a constrained platform generator. Place each new platform relative to
the previous one and limit horizontal shifts according to the actual jump
physics. Start with authored or strongly constrained safe sequences.

Suggested starting values for playtesting
----------------------------------------
Values below describe the logical game world, not required CSS sizes.

Element                  Current         Proposed starting point
Character size           20 x 20         32-40 wide and 32-40 high
Early platform width     60              100-120
Later platform width     60              Gradually reduce toward 70-90
Early vertical gap       80              55-65
Later vertical gap       80              Gradually increase toward 80-90
Failure consequence      Run ends        Three hearts OR one rescue
First platforms          Fully random    Authored or strongly constrained

Do not adopt all values blindly. Use them to build a friendlier prototype
and adjust them after children play it.


6. ADD GOALS AND VARIETY GRADUALLY
==========================================================================

Current problem
---------------
Once movement is understood, successful jumps feel very similar. The
platforms do not change type, spacing does not progress, and variation
mostly comes from random horizontal positions.

Recommended progression
-----------------------
Tutorial stage:
- Normal platforms only.
- Focus on steering and landing.

Next stage:
- Stars or coins placed along safe routes.
- Simple, visible goals.

Later stage:
- Spring platforms.
- Slowly moving platforms.
- Temporary cloud platforms.
- Checkpoints.

Advanced endless mode:
- Narrower platforms.
- Optional challenges.

Introduce these in stages, not all at once.

Examples of child-friendly goals:
- Reach 10 clouds.
- Collect 5 stars.
- Rescue the bird.

Use concrete goals rather than relying only on an abstract endless score.


7. MAKE SUCCESS FEEL REWARDING
==========================================================================

Current problem
---------------
A landing produces a short synthesized boing, but no landing animation,
particle effect, milestone, or celebration. There is no mute control.

Recommended landing feedback
----------------------------
- Squash the character slightly on landing, then stretch it upward.
- Add a small star or cloud-puff effect.
- Briefly animate the platform.
- Increase a clearly visible platform counter.
- Use a gentle sound, with a sound toggle.
- Consider optional vibration where supported; do not rely on it.
- Celebrate every fifth or tenth landing.

Character direction
-------------------
Replace the orange square with a recognizable character, such as a jelly
creature, frog, rabbit, astronaut, or small monster. Even simple eyes and
facial expressions would make success and failure more expressive.

Art should support readability and feedback, not distract from the next
platform or substitute for fixing controls and layout.


8. CROSS-DEVICE TECHNICAL PRIORITIES
==========================================================================

A. Scale the game uniformly
--------------------------
Keep a consistent logical world, such as 400 x 600, and scale the entire
playfield proportionally to fit the available viewport.

Recommended approach:
- Preserve a 2:3 aspect ratio.
- Use dynamic viewport height, such as 100dvh, rather than relying only
  on height: 100%.
- Account for safe areas using env(safe-area-inset-*).
- Set a sensible maximum display size for tablets and computers.
- Handle resizing and orientation changes.
- Do not squeeze width independently of height.

Choose a deliberate landscape-phone behavior:
Option 1: Fit the portrait game to the available height and put controls
          in the unused side areas.
Option 2: Show a friendly "Turn your device upright" screen.

Either approach is preferable to cropping gameplay silently.

B. Render for high-density screens
----------------------------------
The current canvas backing store remains 400 x 600 regardless of device
pixel ratio. This can leave enlarged text and shapes looking soft.

Resize the backing store using window.devicePixelRatio while preserving
logical game coordinates internally.

C. Make physics independent of display refresh rate
--------------------------------------------------
The current update loop applies movement, gravity, and jumping once per
animation frame. It calls requestAnimationFrame without using elapsed
time to control physics.

As a result, gameplay speed can differ between low-frame-rate devices and
60, 90, or 120 Hz displays.

Use either:
- A fixed 60 Hz physics timestep; or
- Carefully implemented elapsed-time-based movement and physics.

A fixed timestep is a suitable starting approach for keeping jumps and
collisions consistent in this game.

D. Unify touch, mouse, and stylus input
-------------------------------------
Replace separate mouse and touch logic with Pointer Events:
pointerdown, pointermove, pointerup, and pointercancel.

Also:
- Capture the active pointer.
- Reset movement on blur and visibilitychange.
- Handle canceled touches.
- Reset left/right movement flags when a run begins.
- Support A / D alongside the arrow keys.
- Prevent relevant browser keyboard actions only while gameplay is active.

E. Preserve browser accessibility
--------------------------------
The current viewport disables user scaling, and touch-action: none is
applied to the whole document.

Remove maximum-scale=1 and user-scalable=no. Restrict touch behavior only
on the interactive game surface instead of disabling it across the page.


9. IMPROVE THE INTERFACE FOR CHILDREN
==========================================================================

Current problem
---------------
The Home button measured approximately 64 x 28 pixels in the earlier test.
It is small and sits inside the left side of the gameplay area, where a
player may press while trying to move.

It also remains visible behind the translucent start screen, although the
overlay blocks interaction.

Recommended interface
---------------------
- Put a large pause button in a safe corner.
- Add a sound toggle.
- Show hearts or rescue tokens when that feature is implemented.
- Show height or platform count clearly.
- Ask for confirmation before leaving an active run.
- Aim for buttons approximately 48-56 CSS pixels high, with generous
  spacing. Treat this as a design target for this prototype.

Use device-neutral wording:
- "Play" instead of "Tap to Start."
- "Play Again" instead of "Tap to Restart."

Example end-of-run screen:

    Almost there!
    You reached 12 clouds.
    Best: 18.

    [ Play Again ]

Keep the tone encouraging and show what the player achieved.


10. PROPOSED FIRST PLAYABLE VERSION
==========================================================================

This is a candidate scope, not a requirement to build everything at once.

1) A friendly animated character around 36 logical pixels in size.
2) A tested touch control scheme, visible guidance, and keyboard support.
3) Five guaranteed tutorial platforms.
4) Forgiving failure: test three hearts or a rescue cloud first.
5) Ten-platform mini-levels, followed by an endless mode.
6) Stars on safe routes and an optional bonus star on a harder route.
7) Wide platforms that gradually become narrower.
8) A pause menu with sound and exit controls.
9) A responsive portrait layout and deliberate landscape fallback.
10) A persistent best score and a small selection of unlockable colors.

Choose the smallest set that demonstrates a clear, enjoyable core loop.
Additional platform types and cosmetic rewards can follow that milestone.


11. RECOMMENDED DEVELOPMENT ORDER
==========================================================================

PRIORITY 1: RELIABLE FOUNDATION
- Proportional responsive scaling.
- High-density canvas rendering.
- Fixed-timestep or otherwise frame-rate-independent physics.
- Unified, dependable controls.

PRIORITY 2: A FRIENDLY FIRST EXPERIENCE
- Clear interactive tutorial.
- Safe starting platforms.
- Immediate rewards.
- Forgiving failure.

PRIORITY 3: PERSONALITY AND PROGRESSION
- Character and landing animations.
- Sound settings.
- Collectibles and small goals.
- Gradual difficulty and additional platform types.

PRIORITY 4: BROADER VALIDATION
- Test rotation and resizing.
- Test browser backgrounding and returning to the game.
- Test touch cancellation and interrupted input.
- Check behavior at 30, 60, and 120 Hz or equivalent frame-rate conditions.
- Test real iOS Safari, Android Chrome, tablets, and desktop browsers.

Do basic device checks during development as well; the final validation
pass should not be the first time layout or input is tested.

FINAL TAKEAWAY
==========================================================================

Keep the bouncing concept. First make movement understandable, the layout
reliable, and early success easy to recognize. Then add personality,
progression, and variety around that stronger foundation.
